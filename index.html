About this model

Each agent counts its neighbors and is happy when the share that matches its own color falls inside [minimum-wanted, maximum-wanted]. Unhappy agents move; happy agents stay. When everyone is happy the run halts.

1. The ceiling is the interesting part. Wilensky's original model has only a floor, so agents are never too segregated. Here maximum-wanted below 100 makes an agent unhappy for being too surrounded by its own kind, which can prevent the run from ever settling.

2. Relocation is global, not local. An unhappy agent jumps to a uniformly random vacant patch anywhere on the torus, not to a nearby one. That is a real difference from classic Schelling: there is no gradient of escape, so clusters form purely from who stays put.

3. The default board is Schelling’s, and three different boards get called that. The default here is 16 × 16 with about a fifth of the cells vacant, which is the board Schelling describes building and running by hand with coins, in his own retrospective account. The figures printed in his 1971 article use a 13 × 16 board, which a square lattice cannot represent; Micromotives and Macrobehavior (1978) uses 8 × 8, which the slider reaches. NetLogo’s own 51 × 51 is the NetLogo defaults preset. The two article attributions come from Hegselmann’s history of the model rather than from a reading of the originals.

4. Lattice size changes the model, not just the picture. The radius-2 disc is 13 patches whatever the world size, so on a 51 × 51 torus a neighborhood is 0.5% of the world and on a 16 × 16 torus it is 5% of it. Shrinking the lattice makes each agent's neighborhood a larger share of everything, and makes the random relocation target a much smaller pool, so small worlds are noisier and reach their resting state in fewer ticks.

5. Under Radius, an agent counts itself. turtles in-radius r includes the asking agent, so every agent contributes one guaranteed same-color neighbor. The disc has 5 patches at radius 1 and 13 at radius 2, so the guaranteed self-match lifts the reported % similar by 50/n points: 10 points at the default radius of 1, and about 4 at radius 2. The minimum threshold is correspondingly easier to clear than it looks. Moore neighborhoods exclude the agent itself, so the two definitions are not on the same scale.
